## Question

How much embodied carbon does seismic resilience cost in a form-found concrete shell, and does form-finding that includes seismic loads deliver it more cheaply than thickening a gravity-only shape?

ShellForm extends the seismic form-finding of Michiels and Adriaenssens (2017, 2018) by sizing each form to pass code checks and counting its A1–A3 embodied carbon.

## Approach

- **Design space.** 2 spans × 2 support cases × 3 rise ratios × 2 strategies (gravity-only, seismic-aware) × 7 spectral acceleration levels = 168 sized designs, across a code-independent seismic demand range of 0–0.6 g, with Chinese (GB 50011) and Eurocode 8 sites marked.
- **Sizing.** Each form is thickened in 5 mm steps until it passes its checks, including linear buckling and a 50 mm minimum thickness, then given its reinforcement.
- **Carbon.** A1–A3, reported under two factor sets (GB/T 51366 and ICE).
- **Decision rule.** Pre-registered: a strategy counts as cheaper only if it saves 5 % or more.
- **Record.** Code frozen at tag `freeze-2026-11-09` on ShellBench v0.3.0; every number traces to a file in `results/`. ShellForm never copies a ShellBench value.

## Validation

ShellForm is built on ShellBench's validated, tagged modules, whose shell analysis reproduces the Scordelis–Lo roof and pinched hemisphere benchmarks within 0.4 %. The new seismic form-finding code has its own validation tests; the suite stood at 101 passing tests on 10 November 2026.

## Results

In 79 of 84 cells the two strategies differ in carbon by less than 5 %: no meaningful difference. The other 5 cells (10 m, corner supports, rise 0.15, Sa 0.1–0.5 g) are one 5 mm thickness step next to the buckling limit, and they disappear under every robustness check.

The reason is the mechanism: thickness is set by linear buckling (corner supports) or the 50 mm minimum (edge supports), and steel by the minimum ratio. None of these responds to Sa up to 0.6 g. The form moves; the carbon does not.

## What this does not show

- Shells are studied as isolated roofs on idealised supports, not as part of a building.
- Seismic demand is applied through a code-independent spectral acceleration range; site-specific hazard is not modelled.
- Carbon is cradle-to-gate (A1–A3); formwork and construction are excluded.
- The manuscript is in preparation; the result is a pre-registered finding, not yet peer reviewed.
