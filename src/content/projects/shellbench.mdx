## Question

A thinner floor weighs less. Less weight means smaller seismic forces, which can mean smaller beams, columns and footings. ShellBench follows that chain through the whole building, using real design codes, rather than comparing floors on their own.

The question: how much do thin-shell floors cut the whole-building A1–A3 embodied carbon of a GB 50010 / GB 50011 reinforced concrete frame across 3–8 storeys, and how does the saving change with storey count and seismic intensity once diaphragm, tie and topping needs are met?

## Approach

The study is checked against one real, fully documented building: my own bachelor thesis design. That building is validation case 0, and every model must reproduce it before it is trusted on anything else.

1. **Audit.** I logged 16 input inconsistencies and 21 doubtful printed values in the thesis, each with a recorded decision, and typed eight thesis result tables in as validation targets.
2. **Anchor model.** The thesis frame rebuilt in OpenSeesPy under gravity, wind and earthquake (GB 50011 base-shear method and a 3D response-spectrum check), with GB 50010 member checks, take-off and A1–A3 carbon. Every difference above 10 % from the thesis has a written reason. Released as v0.1.0.
3. **Generator.** Designs a frame to written design rules, including capacity design, for a family of 36 buildings: 3–8 storeys × six peak ground accelerations (0.05–0.40 g). Failures are counted, not dropped.
4. **Shell floor module.** Force-density form-finding and CalculiX shell analysis; a floor library of 72 cases; a diaphragm test (in-plane deflection ≤ 2 × storey drift) and a topping rule.
5. **Pre-registration.** Hypotheses H1–H4 and the analysis plan were fixed on 3 November 2026, before any family result existed.
6. **Surrogate (Paper 2).** A machine-learning surrogate with split-conformal intervals, frozen before evaluation.

## Validation

The shell solver reproduces two standard benchmarks within 0.4 %: the Scordelis–Lo roof (0.9984 of the reference displacement) and the pinched hemisphere (0.9965). The anchor frame reproduces the thesis storey shears and drifts; each difference above 10 % is explained in the anchor report.

## Results

For the anchor building (4 storeys, 0.10 g), floor carbon falls from 57.6 to 47.4 kgCO₂e/m² (17.8 %), and whole-building carbon from 102.1 to 95.7 kgCO₂e/m² (6.2 %). In the shell family run, every successful design passes the diaphragm test with no topping needed (worst ratio 0.0113).

The surrogate, tested on unseen storey counts, reached conformal coverage of 0.733 against a target of 0.88–0.92, and its decision agreement (93.9 %) was below the 97.0 % of simply always choosing the shell. These are reported as found.

## What this does not show

- All family and surrogate numbers are **pre-release**; the hypothesis verdicts are run once on the published dataset.
- Carbon is cradle-to-gate (A1–A3) only, and footings are outside the scope of the anchor comparison.
- The design rules follow the Chinese codes (GB 50010, GB 50011); other codes would change member sizes.
- Floors are compared at screening level; construction, formwork and long-term behaviour are not modelled.
